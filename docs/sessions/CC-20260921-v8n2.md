    - His diagnosis was right, and there were TWO cuts, not one:
      1. `buildIntakeAdapter.toBuildIntake` joined every item in a dimension into ONE string and clipped
         it at `ANSWER_MAX` (4,000 chars). A customer who answered at length lost the tail. It recorded
         `{ value: '(N items)', reason: 'answer over 4000 chars; clipped' }` — THAT something was lost,
         never WHICH. New exported `packItems()` packs a dimension across as many answers as it needs
         (`constraints`, `constraints_2`, …) and never splits an item; a single item over the ceiling is
         still clipped, but reported with its own text rather than a count. `ANSWERS_MAX` raised 20 → 120,
         since it was one-answer-per-dimension against 15 answerable dimensions and could never bind
         before, and now can.
      2. `decomposePrompt.buildDecomposeUserPrompt` sent the tier band as an instruction:
         "Decompose this into 18-24 requirements". A brief stating 35 was told to produce 24 and complied.
         Now: "Capture EVERY requirement stated... Do not merge, drop, generalise or summarise
         requirements to reach a smaller number... if the material states more, include ALL of them",
         with the tier band demoted to a typical-size note. Releases stay the demo unit — "Scope is chosen
         by which release a requirement lands in, never by leaving it out."
    - Also fixed on the way: the story-spread instruction derived its ceiling from the tier MINIMUM
      (`2 * stories[0] / releases`), which becomes wrong once stories follow the requirement count and
      would read as a story cap. It now states the ratio `release_unbalanced` actually enforces (2x the
      mean of the real story count), so the prompt and the gate say the same thing.
